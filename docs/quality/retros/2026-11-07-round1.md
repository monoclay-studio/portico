## 1. What worked

1. **Identity converged under repeated review.**
   - P0 findings went 2 → 1 → 0, and new P0+P1 findings went 10 → 4 → 2.
   - Review 3 re-ran all 20 earlier attacks inverted, and all were refused.
   - It made a 50–100 ms race deterministic with a test-only `pg_sleep` trigger (W-1). That technique is reusable and
     is now in pattern AP-56.
2. **The round-0 prediction came true, before merge.** The integration-step review found three money and
   authorization P1s. **Three of its findings are residuals of fixes merged in round 0** that nobody had re-attacked:
   - E-039: the payments and finance P0-1 fix exempted booking deposits;
   - E-045: the finance fix left an optional scope that widens to every property;
   - E-046: the rates R-05 seal fix was not applied to the sibling evaluators.

   Binding the real ports made them live. This is direct evidence for round-0 improvement 1.
3. **The orchestrator applied all 8 round-0 edits within 4 minutes of merge**, and verified one with a canary.
   C-L1-01 was caught, which closes E-038. It restored the lost evidence. It also caught its own process slips
   (estimated times, broad commits) and wrote the lessons down.
4. **Merge verification caught two defects before they shipped.** Running the e2e suite on non-default ports exposed
   E-053, and real data exposed E-054. The **neutral fallback** added in the backoffice fix round held under real
   data: an unknown value gave a neutral chip, not a crash. A fail-safe design did its job.
5. **The integration-step review declared its coverage without being asked.** Its § 3, "what I checked and what held",
   is SR2-1 in all but name. It also volunteered a gap in L3 itself: the global consistency checks would miss P1-1 and
   P1-3.
6. **The gap strategist acted as a checker of the checkers.** It found under-reported parity rows, a stale
   `COVERAGE.md`, 16 modules without a matrix, and a duplicate requirement prefix (E-060, E-061).
7. **The refiner's decisions were 32 of 32 accepted or adapted** by backoffice/ui, with 0 rejected (`decisions/backoffice.md`).
   Acceptance is not outcome: round 2 measures outcomes.

## 2. What did not work

1. **Fixes are now the main source of defects.**
   - **12 of the 24** round-1 escapes are fix-related: introduced by a fix, left behind by one, or caused by a
     recommendation (RC-4, RC-9).
   - Each identity fix round opened a narrower class: in round 3, a race in the new wipe, and a field missing from the
     new root-change list.
   - Three merged fixes in other modules had residuals that only binding revealed.
2. **Reviewers' own recommendations caused 3 of review 3's 5 P1/P2 findings.**
   - Review 2 listed root-change fields instead of stating the principle (E-048).
   - It made a domain verification it had itself rated P3 the global proof of email ownership (E-050).
   - Its prescribed wipe locks out legitimate SSO-only members (E-051).
   - This is RC-9: nobody attacked the recommendation.
3. **Tests exercise paths without asserting outcomes (RC-10, 4 escapes, including the first L3 escape).**
   - The binding test over-collected 64,000 against a 14,000 debt and asserted nothing.
   - S04's comparison could not prove its claim.
   - "FD-15 … (tested)" was claimed while derived fields leaked.
4. **Contract drift continues (RC-2).**
   - Five modules hold six provenance literals, four of them different from the kernel's. Reservations lacks
     `sandbox`.
   - Integration still declares its own `module_unavailable` error class (E-062).
   - Canary C-L1-02b showed the typecheck catches a kernel enum change only through payments' copy.
   - The lint rule (round-0 edit 5) is queued but not built.
5. **The test environment is not the production environment (RC-11).**
   - Migrations in tests run as the superuser, so RLS-dependent definer logic cannot fail a test (E-052).
   - The e2e suite worked only on port 5180, and Playwright is outside `turbo test` (E-053).
   - The boundary checker misses `sql.raw` (E-059).
6. **The orchestrator passed things on inexactly (RC-12).**
   - Two broad commits swept an unfinished review into `main` (E-057).
   - Timeline times were estimated (E-055).
   - A review brief restated the builder's claim more broadly than the builder had made it (E-043).
   - The timeline logs the integration-step verdict at 17:24, while the reviewer finished at 17:26:52. Either the time
     is estimated, or the orchestrator acted on the unfinished report. **To verify.**
7. **The meta loop had its own escape (E-056).** Round 0 copied the timeline's estimated times, and took costs from a
   cut-off extraction. Both are corrected, with dated notes.
8. **Playbook adoption lagged.** Round 1 started about one hour after the v2 playbooks were written. Every checker in
   it launched on v1 or a bespoke brief, so v2's effect is still unknown.
