## 1. What worked

1. **Independent review with executable attacks.** All 10 first reviews found real defects in branches that reported
   "done, tests green": 17 P0 and 94 P1 (`05-metrics.md`). No P0 or P1 was disputed. 104 of 104 were acted on in the 9
   reviews whose fix rounds are complete (`scorecard.md` § 3.2). Most were **reproduced by tests that passed against
   the builder's code**, and builders turned those probes into regression tests: CE1–CE8 for inventory, PROBE-2…8 for
   integration, and the identity PoCs.
2. **Binding a module to the *real* neighbour in a scratch copy.** This was the most productive technique:
   - payments bound to real finance (P0-1 cross-property posting; P1-5/P1-6);
   - operations bound to real inventory (P1-3/P1-12);
   - reservations' port-versus-provider table became the integration plan.

   These are the cross-module breaks L3 exists to catch, and they were caught before L3 had run.
3. **Reviews said what held, not only what failed.** For example inventory CE4, integration PROBE-1 and PROBE-4,
   payments "What held up", and finance "Confirmed correct". This calibrates trust and keeps fix rounds from
   over-correcting.
4. **Uncertainty was stated honestly.** Rates R-19 was marked "not verified" and turned out to be real (`944ff0f`). The
   finance parity cluster said plainly that it had cited unmerged branches. Readiness levels were kept honest (Stripe
   at R1).
5. **The re-review closed the loop on identity.** It proved that all 11 original attacks were closed. It found a new
   P0 class, and it found the **root cause** of the cache problem (R2-P2-6), which was then fixed at 17:00 (`1be3ddc`).
6. **The black-box tester's independence held.** Its brief and output contain no build context. Its strengths list
   (readiness model, change preview, honest payment wording) became "must survive" constraints (B1–B7). The refiner's
   split into (a) wiring, (b) missing and (c) design stopped anyone redesigning screens that were only simulated. The
   power check was present in 83 of 83 refinements.
7. **Fix cycles were fast.** Eight modules merged in 54 minutes (11:30–12:24). Each was verified on a merged copy
   first.

## 2. What did not work

1. **Fix rounds were re-checked only once.** Identity-plus was the only branch to get a second review. Its fix round
   introduced or left 6 defects, including one P0 (E-001, E-003…E-007). The 14 P0 fixes in the 8 merged modules were
   verified by the orchestrator, but nobody attacked them again. We do not know whether those fixes introduced
   defects of their own. This is the largest *unmeasured* risk.
2. **Cross-module notes were not tracked.** Two reviews flagged contract drift as "notes for the orchestrator":
   - backoffice, 12:02: "provenance alone has four definitions today, and none includes `sandbox`";
   - finance, 11:42: the error codes `module_unavailable` and `module_not_available`.

   Both then broke `main` at merge, 13 and 37 minutes later (E-008, E-011).
3. **The gates were not trustworthy.**
   - A stale cache hid type errors (2 errors, 3 detections).
   - Lint failures were committed through piped output (4 commits).
   - Two shared tools reported false positives, and two tests were flaky or timed out.
   - The gate command differed between briefs.
   - A residual cache hole for `apps/*` is still open (E-038, found this round).
4. **Reviews do not say what they checked.** A dimension that a review never mentions might have been checked and
   found clean, or might have been skipped (`scorecard.md` § 3.3). The same failure class was found in one module and
   not looked for in the next. For example, SCIM was found admin-equivalent, and SSO configuration was not checked the
   same way (E-002).
5. **The UI review's scope was narrower than the personas.** The launch brief named desk widths from 1280 to 1920, so
   the tablet persona was never measured. Forms were never submitted with invalid input, and search results were never
   clicked. The black-box tester then found 7 defects the UI review should have caught (E-025…E-031).
6. **The L4 evidence chain lost evidence.** Only 68 of 76 screenshot citations were valid. The six missing images were
   lost at the hand-off, not by the tester. The refiner cited one invalid image again, and its summary write was
   refused (E-032…E-036).
7. **The parity gate is neither truthful nor complete.**
   - Some rows overclaimed `tested` (E-019).
   - 19–31 % of edge cases were added only after review (E-020…E-022).
   - parity-ops never ran, so **4 built modules have no matrix** (operations, guests, integration, identity), and
     their completion gate cannot run.
8. **Recall is unknown for 8 of the 10 reviews.** Something re-checked the work only for identity-plus (a second
   review) and the UI (the black-box tester). Both came out at about 0.83. No canary has run yet.
9. **Review evidence is not durable.** PoCs for 9 of the 11 reviews live only in the session scratchpad, and the
   chronicle notes that the container is reclaimed.
10. **The owner caught a scope gap** that the checkers should have found: keys and the whole guest stay (E-024).
11. **L6 observation (not a checker escape).** The 5-hour window reached 100 % at 13:40, after the 12:36 checkpoint, "from
    work that was still finishing" (`01-timeline.md`). The record does not say which work. It is noted here for the
    usage loop's owner.
