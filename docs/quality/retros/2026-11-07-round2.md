## 1. The three questions

### (a) Did v2.1's "attack the fix" and the builder's § 3 work? Do the "one step beyond" P1s show a missing technique?

**v2.1 worked, for reviewers.**
- All 3 v2.1 runs declared coverage, launched from the playbook, and re-ran every original attack inverted: 56 in
  the sweep, 9 in the re-review, 1 in guests.
- Every P1 the re-review found came from a named v2.1 step:
  - reverse order (R2-P1-1);
  - sibling and repeat paths (R2-P1-2);
  - the load-bearing re-rating (R2-P1-3).
- Three of the sweep's four P1s came from sibling paths and the load-bearing check. The fourth, a card from another
  property, came from AP-01 applied to a second record parameter.
- Reviewers now write each recommended fix as a principle with instances. Example: "a departed folio has at most one
  open balance request, and it always equals the folio's debt".
- The one v2.1 miss traces to **my pattern wording, not to the playbook.** AP-56's examples did not name erasure, so
  the sweep marked it not applicable. A write racing an erasure then survived (E-083).

**§ 3 was adopted, but it is not sufficient: a technique is missing.**
- All 3 fix rounds filled the table, and identity round 4 added genuine reverse-order and sibling regression tests.
- Yet the integration-step round still left 3 P1s one step beyond its fixes. The sweep found 8 residuals of round-0
  fixes, and the guests fix introduced 3 new P2s.

Read side by side, the misses share one shape (RC-13): **the fix guards a path; the defect lives in the invariant.**
§ 3(b) "siblings" was read as *other callers of the guarded action*. Nobody enumerated these:

| Missed dimension | Escapes |
|---|---|
| **Writers of the facts the fix trusts** (premise, step N−1) | R2-P1-1 (booker and occupant links), R2-P1-3 (opening-deposit import), SW-PAY-4 (`entry_channel`), SW-GUE-1 (which stores "erased" covers), the guests redaction identifier (E-082) |
| **Repeat and inverse operations** (step N+1) | R2-P1-2 (second charge; reversal), SW-FIN-1 (rule edit) |
| **The fix's own records** | R2-P1-2 (exception severity frozen at "routine") |
| **Other record parameters and stores** | SW-PAY-3 (the card), SW-GUE-1 (`core.idempotency_keys`) |
| **Exemptions the fix adds** | R2-P2-3 (`return`s), SW-PAY-2 (`custom`), SW-RAT-1 (default) |
| **Tests that pass without the fix** | SW-BO-1 (reload), R2-P2-4 (asserts `done`) |

**The missing technique is invariant closure.**
1. State each fix as an invariant over **stored state**.
2. Enumerate every **writer** of every input of that invariant, including imports, channels, merges, reversals,
   transfers and jobs.
3. Apply the operation three times, and apply every inverse operation.
4. Recompute the fix's own records.
5. List the exemptions.
6. Prove that each regression test **fails without the fix**.

This is published as **builder self-check v4 § 5** (BS4-1 to BS4-3), with patterns AP-57 and AP-58 and canaries
C-L2-18 and C-L2-19. Reviewers already do this through steps 5 and 6, so steward v2.1 is unchanged. The sweep's
suggestion of "one test per sibling entry point" is included, generalized from entry points to **writers**.

### (b) What mechanism would stop estimated timestamps for good?

**Data.**
- Rounds 0–1: more than 10 times off by 10–60 minutes (E-055).
- Round 2: 4 more, off by 1–6 minutes (E-080), each corrected within minutes.
- The O2-4 *rule* shrank the error but did not remove it.

**Root cause.** Two things combine:
- the authoritative record is not where the orchestrator writes. `agents.json` is refreshed only at extraction, and
  the "refreshed agent records" commits show the extract had to be re-run before each correction;
- typing a time is the path of least resistance.

**The helper you propose is necessary, but not sufficient on its own.** It needs three properties:
1. **It reads the live source, not the extract.** The prototype `<scratchpad>/canary-scripts/when.py` reads the
   subagent transcript's last assistant message, the commit time, or `date -u`. It printed the correct times for the
   sweep (18:15, first typed 18:13), the re-review (18:29, first typed 18:30), the parity merge (18:02, first typed
   18:08) and integration review 1 (17:26:51). Note that the timeline still says 17:27 there: a **floor-to-minute**
   rounding convention is needed.
2. **It emits a source token** with each time: `<!-- t: agent-… @ ISO -->` or `commit:<sha>`.
3. **A verifier makes it a gate.** `tools/commit-checked.sh` runs `verify-times.py` whenever `01-timeline.md` is
   staged. Each new bullet must carry a token, and its HH:MM must equal the floor of the source time; otherwise the
   commit is refused.

The helper prevents errors, and the verifier detects them. Together they turn a habit into a mechanism, which is
orchestrator v3 **O3-1**. Measure: timeline corrections per round, target 0.
